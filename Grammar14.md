# Grammar: Partizip I und Partizip II als Adjektive

### 1. Partizip I — formation

**Infinitiv + d**. That's it — no exceptions.

| Infinitiv | Partizip I |
| --- | --- |
| lachen | lachend |
| schlafen | schlafend |
| lesen | lesend |
| warten | wartend |
| ankommen | ankommend |

### 2. Partizip II — formation (recap)

| Verb type | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| schwach (regular) | ge- + stem + -t | machen → gemacht |
| stark (irregular) | ge- + (often changed) stem + -en | lesen → gelesen |
| untrennbar (be-, ver-, er-, ent-, emp-, ge-, miss-, zer-) | no ge- | verstehen → verstanden |
| trennbar | ge- between prefix and stem | aufmachen → aufgemacht (see [Grammar12](Grammar12.md)) |
| verbs ending in -ieren | no ge- | studieren → studiert |

---

### 3. Using them as adjectives

Both Partizipien work exactly like any other adjective: they go before a noun and take normal adjective endings ([Grammar0](Grammar0.md) — the endings don't change just because it's a Partizip).

**Same verb, both Partizipien:**

| Verb | Partizip I as adjective | Partizip II as adjective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| lesen | der **lesende** Mann (the man who is reading) | das **gelesene** Buch (the book that has been read) |
| kochen | die **kochende** Frau (the woman who is cooking) | die **gekochten** Kartoffeln (the potatoes that have been cooked) |
| ankommen | der **ankommende** Zug (the train that is arriving) | der **angekommene** Zug (the train that has arrived) |

---

### 4. Gegenwart/Vergangenheit — what each one actually means

| Partizip | Voice | Timing (relative to the noun's context) | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Partizip I | Aktiv | **gleichzeitig** — happening right now, ongoing | der lesende Mann = der Mann, der gerade liest |
| Partizip II (transitives Verb) | Passiv | **vorzeitig** — already completed | das gelesene Buch = das Buch, das gelesen wurde |
| Partizip II (intransitives sein-Verb) | Aktiv | **vorzeitig** — already completed, but not passive | der angekommene Zug = der Zug, der angekommen ist |

The third row is the one people forget: with intransitive verbs that take sein (ankommen, sterben, fallen, aufstehen ...), there's no object to passivize, so Partizip II just means "already happened" — not "was done to."

---

### 5. Common traps

- **Partizip I is never passive.** "der lesende Mann" only ever means "the man who reads/is reading" — never "the man who is being read." (English "-ing" can be ambiguous here in a way German isn't.)
- **Don't confuse it with the substantivized infinitive.** "das Lesen" (the noun "reading") vs. "der lesende Mann" (Partizip I adjective) — similar root, completely different job in the sentence.
- **Partizip II's meaning depends on transitivity.** Transitive verb → passive ("das gelesene Buch" = read *by someone*). Intransitive sein-verb → just "completed," no passive sense ("der angekommene Zug").
- **Life-and-death trap:** der **sterbende** Mann (the dying man — still alive, ongoing) vs. der **gestorbene** Mann (the deceased man — already dead, completed). Easy to blur in translation; the meaning gap is huge in German.
- **Semantic drift:** some Partizip-I adjectives picked up a life of their own and don't mean "currently doing X" anymore — reizend (charming, not "currently irritating"), spannend (exciting, not "currently stretching"), entscheidend (decisive/crucial, not "currently deciding").

### 6. Plural endings — checking your rule

Your rule: *no article → only -e, with article → -en.*

That's **correct for Nominativ and Akkusativ Plural** — but it breaks for Dativ and Genitiv. Full picture (from [Grammar0](Grammar0.md)):

| Fall | ohne Artikel (Nullartikel) | mit bestimmtem Artikel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nominativ | **-e** (lesende Männer) | **-en** (die lesenden Männer) |
| Akkusativ | **-e** (lesende Männer) | **-en** (die lesenden Männer) |
| Dativ | **-en** (mit lesenden Männern) | **-en** (mit den lesenden Männern) |
| Genitiv | **-er** (lesender Männer) | **-en** (der lesenden Männer) |

So: your -e/-en split holds for **Nom./Akk. only**. Dativ Plural is **always -en** regardless of article, and Genitiv Plural without an article is **-er**, not -e.
