# Grammar: Genitiv-Relativsätze (dessen/deren)

dessen and deren mean "whose" — they replace a genitive (possessive) noun and turn two sentences into one relative clause.

### The one rule that matters

**dessen/deren agree with the ANTECEDENT** (the person/thing before the comma — the "owner") — **never** with the noun that comes right after them (the thing being owned).

| Antecedent is... | Use | 
| --- | --- |
| maskulin | **dessen** |
| neutrum | **dessen** |
| feminin | **deren** |
| Plural | **deren** |

> Bonus: dessen/deren never change further, no matter what case the noun after it needs for its own role in the clause. "dessen Auto" stays "dessen Auto" whether Auto is Nominativ, Akkusativ, or Dativ in that clause.

Every example below deliberately pairs an antecedent with a possessed noun of a **different** gender — so you can see for yourself that dessen/deren tracks the antecedent, not the noun sitting right next to it.

---

### 1. Maskulines Antezedens → dessen

**A. Two separate sentences:**
Der Mann wohnt neben mir. Sein Auto ist rot.

**B. With a normal genitive:**
Das Auto **des Mannes** ist rot.

**C. As a genitive relative clause:**
<span style="color:blue">**Der Mann**</span>, <span style="color:blue">**dessen**</span> Auto rot ist, wohnt neben mir.

`dessen ←──────── agrees with "der Mann" (maskulin) — NOT with "Auto" (neutrum)`

---

### 2. Feminines Antezedens → deren

**A. Two separate sentences:**
Die Frau arbeitet im Krankenhaus. Ihr Sohn studiert Medizin.

**B. With a normal genitive:**
Der Sohn **der Frau** studiert Medizin.

**C. As a genitive relative clause:**
<span style="color:red">**Die Frau**</span>, <span style="color:red">**deren**</span> Sohn Medizin studiert, arbeitet im Krankenhaus.

`deren ←──────── agrees with "die Frau" (feminin) — NOT with "Sohn" (maskulin)`

---

### 3. Neutrales Antezedens → dessen

**A. Two separate sentences:**
Das Unternehmen wächst schnell. Sein Erfolg beeindruckt alle.

**B. With a normal genitive:**
Der Erfolg **des Unternehmens** beeindruckt alle.

**C. As a genitive relative clause:**
<span style="color:green">**Das Unternehmen**</span>, <span style="color:green">**dessen**</span> Erfolg alle beeindruckt, wächst schnell.

`dessen ←──────── agrees with "das Unternehmen" (neutrum) — NOT with "Erfolg" (maskulin)`

---

### 4. Plural-Antezedens → deren

**A. Two separate sentences:**
Die Kinder spielen im Garten. Ihre Spielzeuge liegen überall.

**B. With a normal genitive:**
Die Spielzeuge **der Kinder** liegen überall.

**C. As a genitive relative clause:**
**Die Kinder**, **deren** Spielzeuge überall liegen, spielen im Garten.

`deren ←──────── agrees with "die Kinder" (Plural) — NOT with "Spielzeuge" (also Plural here, but watch out: this would still be deren even if the possessed noun were singular/a different gender)`

---

> **Why this trips people up:** with a normal relative pronoun (der/die/das), the pronoun's gender comes from the antecedent too — so that part's the same. The difference is dessen/deren stay genitive-fixed no matter what role the following noun plays, while der/die/das change case depending on that role. Two sentences, one rule: look **left**, past the comma, to the antecedent — never right.
