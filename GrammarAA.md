# Grammar: entweder...oder / weder...noch (either...or / neither...nor)

Both are two-part connectors that can join almost any kind of element — nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, or full clauses. English "either...or" / "neither...nor" don't affect word order at all; German does, and that's the part worth memorizing carefully.

### entweder ... oder — either ... or

Presents two alternatives; usually implies you pick one, not both.

### weder ... noch — neither ... nor

Rejects both alternatives at once. It's already negative on its own — never add nicht or kein on top of it (that would be a double negative).

---

### Hard and fast rules

1. **Joining small elements (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) inside one clause:** no word-order change at all. Just drop "entweder X oder Y" / "weder X noch Y" straight into the normal sentence.
2. **Joining two full clauses — entweder/oder:** if entweder opens the first clause, it occupies position 1 and forces **inversion** (verb before subject) in that clause. oder itself is a true "zero-position" coordinator (like und, aber, denn) — it does **not** trigger inversion in the second clause, which keeps normal subject-verb order.
3. **Joining two full clauses — weder/noch:** same story for weder in the first clause. But **noch is not a zero-position word** — it behaves like an adverb sitting in position 1, so it **always forces inversion** in its own clause. This is the opposite behavior from oder, and it's the detail most learners get wrong.
4. entweder and weder don't have to sit at the very start of the clause — they can sit later, in the middle field, right before the items they connect. When they're not in position 1, there's simply nothing to invert (see the two clause-level rows in the table below for the contrast).

| | oder | noch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Triggers inversion in the clause it starts? | Never | Always |

### entweder ... oder — examples by connected item

| Connects | German | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nouns | Ich trinke entweder Kaffee oder Tee. | I drink either coffee or tea. |
| Verbs | Er wird entweder kommen oder anrufen. | He will either come or call. |
| Adjectives | Das Auto ist entweder rot oder blau. | The car is either red or blue. |
| Adverbs | Wir treffen uns entweder heute oder morgen. | We'll meet either today or tomorrow. |
| Clauses, entweder in position 1 (→ inversion) | Entweder kommst du mit, oder du bleibst hier. | Either you come along, or you stay here. |
| Clauses, entweder in the middle field (no inversion) | Du kommst entweder mit, oder du bleibst hier. | You either come along, or you stay here. |

### weder ... noch — examples by connected item

| Connects | German | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nouns | Ich mag weder Kaffee noch Tee. | I like neither coffee nor tea. |
| Verbs | Er kann weder schwimmen noch tauchen. | He can neither swim nor dive. |
| Adjectives | Das Wetter war weder warm noch kalt. | The weather was neither warm nor cold. |
| Adverbs | Sie kommt weder heute noch morgen. | She's coming neither today nor tomorrow. |
| Clauses, weder in position 1 (both clauses invert — noch always does) | Weder hat er angerufen, noch hat er geschrieben. | He neither called nor wrote. |
| Clauses, weder in the middle field (noch still inverts its own clause) | Er hat weder angerufen, noch hat er geschrieben. | He neither called nor wrote. |

---

### entweder ... oder in questions (Alternativfrage)

German yes/no questions already put the verb first (V1: "Hast du es getan?"). Statement-inversion after entweder (position 1) also produces verb-before-subject order — so the two word orders look identical on the surface. That coincidence is exactly why sticking "entweder" onto a question is grammatical but not how it's actually done.

| Version | German | English | Natural? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alternativfrage (idiomatic) | Hast du es getan, oder hat er es getan? | Did you do it, or did he do it? | ✅ standard |
| With entweder up front | Entweder hast du es getan, oder hat er es getan? | Either you did it, or did he do it? | technically grammatical, reads as marked/unusual |

> **Rule:** to ask "either...or" as a real question, just join two normal V1 questions with plain **oder** — drop entweder. It isn't needed, because oder alone already signals the choice; entweder's only job elsewhere is to force inversion in statements, and questions are already inverted.
