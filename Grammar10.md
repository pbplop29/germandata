# Grammar: wer/wen/wem ... der/den/dem (generalizing statements)

### When do you use this?

When the person is **unknown or not specified** — you're making a general statement about "whoever does X ..." Common in proverbs and general rules.

### Structure

**[Wer/Wen/Wem ... verb (at the end)]**, **[der/den/dem + verb + rest]**

Part 1 behaves like a subordinate clause (verb pushed to the end). Part 2 is a completely normal main clause — der/den/dem just sits in position 1, then the verb (position 2), then everything else.

---

### The 3 cases — how to spot which one you need

| Case | Question | Part 1 | Part 2 | How reliable is it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nominative | Who/what does the action? | wer | der | Default — it's just the subject, no special signal |
| Accusative | Who/what is directly affected? | wen | den | Check if the verb takes a plain direct object |
| Dative | To/for whom? | wem | dem | **Most reliable case to spot** — see the signal list below |

> Part 1's case and Part 2's case are picked **completely independently** — look at each half's own verb separately. They usually don't match.

---

### Dative signals — the reliable "always" list

Dative is the one case with clear, memorizable triggers. If you see any of these, it's Dativ — don't overthink it.

**A. Verbs that always take Dativ (never a plain Akkusativ object):**

helfen (help), danken (thank), gratulieren (congratulate), folgen (follow), glauben + person (believe someone), gehören (belong to), gefallen (please/like), schmecken (taste good to), passen (fit/suit), antworten (answer), vertrauen (trust), zuhören (listen to), zusehen (watch), widersprechen (contradict), fehlen (be missing/lacking to), gelingen (succeed for), auffallen (stand out to), drohen (threaten), raten (advise), verzeihen (forgive), begegnen (meet/encounter), dienen (serve), schaden (harm), ähneln (resemble), genügen (be enough for), zustimmen (agree with)

**B. Impersonal "es/das + verb + Dativ" expressions — always Dativ:**

Das gefällt mir. (I like that.) · Das schmeckt mir. (That tastes good to me.) · Das passt mir. (That suits me.) · Das macht mir Spaß. (That's fun for me.) · Es tut mir leid. (I'm sorry.) · Es geht mir gut. (I'm doing well.)
*(same list already covered in [Grammar3](Grammar3.md) §4 — Feste Wendungen mit es)*

**C. Adjective + sein + Dativ — "it is ... to me":**

Das ist mir wichtig/egal/peinlich/klar/bewusst/kalt/warm/zu teuer/langweilig. (That is important/doesn't matter/embarrassing/clear/cold/too expensive/boring to me.)

**D. Prepositions that always take Dativ:**

aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu, gegenüber, außer

### All 9 combinations, with examples

| Part 1 case | Part 2 case | German | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nominative | Nominative | Wer zu spät kommt, der muss warten. | Whoever is late has to wait. |
| Nominative | Accusative | Wer lügt, den bestraft das Leben. | Whoever lies gets punished by life. |
| Nominative | Dative | Wer zu spät kommt, dem hilft niemand. | Whoever is late — nobody helps them. |
| Accusative | Nominative | Wen ich liebe, der ist mir wichtig. | Whoever I love is important to me. |
| Accusative | Accusative | Wen du einlädst, den musst du auch bezahlen. | Whoever you invite, you also have to pay for. |
| Accusative | Dative | Wen du kennst, dem kannst du vertrauen. | Whoever you know, you can trust. |
| Dative | Nominative | Wem ich vertraue, der wird nicht enttäuscht. | Whoever I trust won't be disappointed. |
| Dative | Accusative | Wem ich helfe, den unterstütze ich auch finanziell. | Whoever I help, I also support financially. |
| Dative | Dative | Wem ich vertraue, dem glaube ich auch. | Whoever I trust, I also believe. |
