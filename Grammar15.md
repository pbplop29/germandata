# Grammar: Kausale Präpositionen (wegen, aufgrund, dank, aus, vor)

### Overview

| Preposition | Case | Type of reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| wegen | Genitiv | neutral reason |
| aufgrund | Genitiv | neutral reason (more formal register than wegen) |
| dank | Genitiv | reason with a **positive** outcome |
| aus | Dativ | emotional reason, but the reaction is **controllable** — a deliberate action |
| vor | Dativ | emotional reason, and the reaction is **uncontrollable** — an involuntary/physical response |

---

### 1. wegen + Genitiv — neutral reason

The default, everyday word for "because of." Carries no judgment about whether the reason is good or bad.

1. Wegen des schlechten Wetters fällt der Ausflug aus. (Because of the bad weather, the trip is cancelled.)
2. Wegen der Baustelle mussten wir einen Umweg fahren. (Because of the construction site, we had to take a detour.)
3. Wegen seiner Krankheit konnte er nicht zur Arbeit kommen. (Because of his illness, he couldn't come to work.)

> In casual spoken German you'll often hear "wegen dem Wetter" (Dativ) — but Genitiv ("wegen des Wetters") is the correct/written form and what's expected at B2.

### 2. aufgrund + Genitiv — neutral reason, formal register

Same meaning as wegen, but sounds more official/bureaucratic — typical of news reports, business writing, official notices.

1. Aufgrund der aktuellen Lage wurde die Veranstaltung abgesagt. (Due to the current situation, the event was cancelled.)
2. Aufgrund mangelnder Erfahrung wurde er nicht eingestellt. (Due to a lack of experience, he wasn't hired.)
3. Aufgrund des Streiks fahren heute keine Busse. (Due to the strike, no buses are running today.)

### 3. dank + Genitiv — reason with a positive impact

Use dank when the reason led to a **good** result — it carries a sense of gratitude/credit, unlike the neutral wegen.

1. Dank deiner Hilfe habe ich die Prüfung bestanden. (Thanks to your help, I passed the exam.)
2. Dank des guten Wetters konnten wir den ganzen Tag draußen bleiben. (Thanks to the good weather, we could stay outside all day.)
3. Dank ihres Einsatzes wurde das Projekt rechtzeitig fertig. (Thanks to her commitment, the project was finished on time.)

### 4. aus + Dativ — emotional reason, controllable (a deliberate action)

The feeling motivates an action the person **chooses** to do. Test: can you rephrase it as "he/she decided to do X because they felt Y"? If yes, it's aus.

1. Sie hat ihm aus Mitleid geholfen. (She helped him out of pity.)
2. Er hat die Wahrheit aus Liebe verschwiegen. (He kept the truth secret out of love.)
3. Ich habe die Frage nur aus Neugier gestellt. (I only asked the question out of curiosity.)

### 5. vor + Dativ — emotional reason, uncontrollable (an involuntary reaction)

The feeling triggers something that just **happens to** the person — a physical/instinctive response, not a decision. Test: is the verb something you can't consciously control (zittern, weinen, erstarren, schreien)? If yes, it's vor.

1. Sie zitterte vor Angst. (She trembled from fear.)
2. Er konnte vor Freude nicht schlafen. (He couldn't sleep from joy.)
3. Das Kind weinte vor Schmerz. (The child cried from pain.)

---

> **Quick test for aus vs. vor:** aus + Dativ → "I did X because I felt Y" (an action). vor + Dativ → "Y made my body do X" (a reaction). Same emotion can sometimes take either, depending on which one you mean: "aus Angst" (she fled the country out of fear — a decision) vs. "vor Angst" (she trembled from fear — a reaction).
