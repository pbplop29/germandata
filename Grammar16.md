# Grammar: sodass, so...dass, indem, dadurch dass (vs. weil, damit, deshalb)

Quick map before the details: **sodass/so...dass** state a **result** ("with the consequence that..."). **indem/dadurch dass** state the **means/method** ("by doing..."). Neither is technically "causal" like weil — but they're grouped together here because German learners mix them up with weil/damit/deshalb constantly, for good reason: real-world cause and effect often gets expressed with any of these depending on which side of it you're emphasizing.

---

### 1. sodass / so dass — Konsekutivsatz (result)

**Structure:** Hauptsatz, + sodass + Nebensatz (Verb am Ende). ("sodass" is one word in modern spelling; "so dass" as two words is still accepted.)

Says: X happened, **and as a result**, Y happened. No judgment on whether Y was intended.

1. Es regnete stark, sodass wir zu Hause blieben. (It rained heavily, so we stayed home.)
2. Er hat hart gearbeitet, sodass er die Prüfung bestanden hat. (He worked hard, so he passed the exam.)
3. Der Zug hatte Verspätung, sodass ich den Anschlusszug verpasste. (The train was late, so I missed the connecting train.)

### 2. so ... dass — same idea, but naming the degree

**Structure:** Hauptsatz mit so + Adjektiv/Adverb ..., dass + Nebensatz (Verb am Ende). "so" sits directly in front of the adjective/adverb that caused the result.

Use this instead of plain sodass when you want to say **how much** of something led to the result.

1. Es regnete so stark, dass wir zu Hause blieben. (It rained so heavily that we stayed home.)
2. Er hat so hart gearbeitet, dass er die Prüfung bestanden hat. (He worked so hard that he passed the exam.)
3. Der Kaffee war so heiß, dass ich mir die Zunge verbrannt habe. (The coffee was so hot that I burned my tongue.)

---

### 3. indem — Modalsatz (by doing X)

**Structure:** Hauptsatz, + indem + Nebensatz (Verb am Ende). Usually the same subject (or closely tied agent) in both clauses.

Test: can you rephrase it as "by doing ..."? If yes, indem fits.

1. Er lernte Deutsch, indem er jeden Tag Vokabeln übte. (He learned German by practicing vocabulary every day.)
2. Sie hat Geld gespart, indem sie weniger ausgegangen ist. (She saved money by going out less.)
3. Man kann Energie sparen, indem man das Licht ausschaltet. (You can save energy by turning off the light.)

### 4. dadurch, dass — same job as indem, more flexible

**Structure:** Hauptsatz mit dadurch (Platzhalter) + , dass + Nebensatz. Works even when the two clauses have **different subjects** — indem gets awkward there.

1. Er lernte Deutsch dadurch, dass er jeden Tag Vokabeln übte. (same meaning as the indem example — more formal/explicit)
2. Die Firma sparte Geld dadurch, dass die Mitarbeiter weniger Überstunden machten. (The company saved money by the employees working less overtime — different subjects, indem would be clumsy here.)
3. Der Unfall wurde dadurch verursacht, dass der Fahrer zu schnell fuhr. (The accident was caused by the driver driving too fast.)

---

### 5. vs. weil — cause, opposite direction

weil answers "why?" and states the **cause**; sodass answers "with what result?" and states the **consequence**. Same real-world facts, opposite grammatical framing:

| weil (cause-first) | sodass (result-first) |
| --- | --- |
| Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil es regnet. | Es regnet, sodass ich zu Hause bleibe. |

### 6. vs. damit — the classic exam trap: intention vs. result

damit = **intended** purpose (a goal you want). sodass = **actual** consequence (may be unplanned/incidental). Same sentence, different word, different meaning:

| damit (I want this outcome) | sodass (this outcome just happens) |
| --- | --- |
| Ich schließe das Fenster, damit es warm bleibt. (closing it so that it *will* stay warm — that's my goal) | Ich schließe das Fenster, sodass es warm bleibt. (I close it, and it happens to stay warm as a result) |
| Sie spricht langsam, damit die Kinder sie verstehen. (speaking slowly on purpose, so the kids *can* understand) | Sie spricht langsam, sodass die Kinder sie verstehen. (she speaks slowly, and as a result they understand) |

### 7. vs. deshalb — not a conjunction, an adverb

deshalb doesn't create a subordinate clause — it starts a **new main clause** (or sits inside one), so the verb stays in **position 2** (inversion), not at the end.

| sodass (one sentence, subordinate clause) | deshalb (two main clauses) |
| --- | --- |
| Es regnete stark, sodass wir zu Hause blieben. | Es regnete stark. Deshalb blieben wir zu Hause. |
